## Intended use

Decision support for mortgage underwriting: estimate how likely a lender would be to deny an application and **refer** high-likelihood applications to an underwriter with the main reasons. Operating rule: refer when p(denial) >= threshold; threshold set on 2024 to catch 80% of denials (threshold 0.1382).

Not intended to deny credit automatically, to price loans, or to predict repayment: the label is the lender's decision, not loan performance.

### Less discriminatory alternative search, joint (2024 validation year)

Each variant is the v1 recipe with one change to the training loss; protected attributes enter only the loss, never the inputs or the threshold (ADR 0010). Three variants:

- **Reweighing** (Kamiran & Calders 2012): each application weighted so that the denial rate is the same in every age x race x sex cell.
- **Mean-logit penalty**: cross-entropy + lambda x the squared gap in mean logit between each group and its reference, among approved and among denied applications separately (a relaxation of equalized odds, Hardt et al. 2016), for age 62+, Black and female applicants. lambda = 0 is the v1 recipe re-run.
- **Referral-rate penalty**: the same, on a smooth referral rate at each batch's own 80%-recall threshold, with American Indian applicants added (split-halves estimate for small groups). Equal referral rates among approved and among denied applications would give every group whose denial rate is higher an AIR at least the lenders' own.

Of the 5 groups v1 fails, the chosen variant passes 5. `race:Asian` is flagged at an AIR of 1.002, at or above parity with its reference: the audit flags any model AIR more than 0.02 below the lenders' own (1.036). Equalized odds moves a group whose denial rate is lower than its reference's toward parity (the inequality in `ldp.training.lda` reverses for it), so for such groups this rule and this method pull against each other. Whether the rule should apply to a group at or above parity is for the audit's owner to decide; it is not changed here after seeing the 2025 result.

## Explanations

On 20,000 denied 2025 applications where the lender's first reported reason was debt-to-income or collateral, that reason was the model's top reason for 71.1% and among its top four for 93.0%.

## Stability

Score PSI against the 2023 training population: 0.004 on 2024, 0.016 on 2025. Largest input shifts on 2025: `applicant_credit_score_type` 0.174, `loan_purpose` 0.153, `income` 0.093.

## Limitations

- HMDA has no credit score, credit history or employment data. The model sees only loan and income characteristics, so it cannot weigh what an underwriter weighs most, and its reasons can never cite credit.
- The label is the lenders' decision. The model learns their practices, including any disparities in them; the fairness audit compares the model with those decisions but cannot make the labels fair.
- HMDA rounds loan amounts and property values to $10,000 midpoints and bins debt-to-income ratios.
- Applications are not individual consumers' credit files; protected attributes are applicant-reported (or observed) HMDA fields, used only for monitoring.
- A threshold that catches 80% of denials is a policy choice; the referral volume it implies has to fit underwriting capacity.
